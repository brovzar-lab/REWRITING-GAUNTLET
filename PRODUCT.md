# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Individual screenwriters rewriting feature films, one-hour television pilots, and half-hour television pilots in English, Spanish, or both. They may be professionals working under pressure or serious writers developing a draft over time. Their central task is to diagnose and strengthen an existing screenplay without losing its voice, intent, or authorship.

## Product Purpose

Rewrite Studio turns Jack Epps Jr.'s rewriting methodology into a complete working environment for screenplay revision. It combines a professional screenplay editor, a tactile visual story board, evidence-backed diagnosis, human and AI notes, and repeatable rewrite passes. Success means the writer always understands what the screenplay is doing, what needs attention, why a change is proposed, and which approved changes have entered the draft.

The screenplay editor is the center of the product. The board, maps, notes, and AI assistance support the screenplay rather than replacing it. AI works as a visible collaborator with evidence and provenance. It does not silently take authorship or produce an unreviewed one-shot page-one rewrite.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, tactile, focused. The product should feel like a premium professional writers' room joined to a film editing suite. It should communicate serious craft, creative momentum, and calm authority. The experience is immersive and expressive without becoming theatrical, distracting, or precious.

## Anchor References

- A professional film editing suite for atmosphere, focus, and purposeful density.
- A physical writers' room for tactile index cards, spatial story thinking, and visible relationships.
- Final Draft for familiar screenplay writing, formatting, pagination, keyboard flow, scene navigation, and revision behavior.
- Figma for interaction clarity, direct manipulation, and dependable tool behavior.

## Anti-references

The product must not resemble a generic AI dashboard, oversized marketing page, chatbot-first workflow, playful startup tool, or school exercise. Avoid excessive empty space, repetitive rounded-card grids, inflated typography, decorative AI imagery, and interfaces that hide the screenplay behind analysis. Do not make writers learn an invented editing model when established screenplay conventions already work.

## Design Principles

1. **The page is the center.** Every tool returns the writer to concrete screenplay text, scenes, beats, and approved revisions.
2. **Make story structure tangible.** Cards, maps, connections, and rewrite passes should feel directly manipulable and spatially understandable.
3. **Show the reasoning.** Separate textual facts, reader reactions, AI hypotheses, writer-confirmed interpretations, and unresolved questions.
4. **Protect authorship.** Preserve voice, lock unaffected scenes, track provenance, and require approval before proposed writing enters the draft.
5. **Support flow under pressure.** Use familiar editing conventions, compact hierarchy, keyboard-first operation, and progressive detail so serious work feels fast.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.1 AA as the baseline. Support complete keyboard navigation, visible focus states, reduced motion, adjustable screenplay zoom, and sufficient contrast. Never communicate meaning through color alone. The interface and all core workflows must support English, Spanish, and bilingual screenplays, including longer translated interface text and mixed-language content.
